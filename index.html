My Family, My First Team

I have a twin brother named Jesús Alberto Turizo Hernández, and growing up with him has always felt like having a partner for everything. We share many memories, and we also share the experience of studying at the same time, which is not always easy but keeps us motivated.

My family has always supported my studies. When I decided to study Informatics Engineering and, later, to join the ADSO program at SENA, my parents encouraged me to keep learning, even when I had to manage classes, projects, and work at the same time. I think that a supportive family is the first team a person can have, because they are the people who believe in you before anyone else does.

"Family is not only about living together; it is about growing together, too."

Today, I try to make my family proud by finishing my projects and by building a better future through technology. Every achievement I reach belongs a little bit to all of them.

Building Worlds: My Passion for Games and Code

One of my biggest interests is game development. I enjoy designing small browser games, thinking about the rules, the characters, and how a player will feel while playing. For me, a good game is like a small world that someone else can visit and enjoy.

I also love web design. Through my personal project, "El Mundo de Manu," I have created different sections about technology, gaming, education, and other topics that interest me. I consider that building your own digital space teaches you much more than any tutorial, because you have to solve real problems by yourself.

In my free time, I usually study new programming techniques, watch tutorials about 3D animations, or sketch ideas for a new game. These hobbies are not just entertainment for me; they are also practice for my future career as a developer.

Travel — A touristic place in my region
Post 4 of 7

El Cristo Petrolero: A Symbol of My Hometown

One of the most impressive places in my region is the Cristo Petrolero, located on the Malecón, next to the Miramar swamp, in Barrancabermeja. It stands right in front of the biggest oil refinery in the country, and it is a huge metallic statue, 26 meters tall, with its arms open to the sky.

The statue was designed by Fernando Fernández, a refinery worker, and it was built together with 20 other workers. It was inaugurated in 1995 and donated by Ecopetrol to make the city more beautiful. At night, the lights and the water fountain around it make the whole place look magical, and during the day, you can walk along a path with metal dinosaur figures and typical swamp vegetation.

"A city built around the oil industry, but also full of art, nature, and history."

I chose this place because it represents the identity of my region. I believe every visitor should see it at least once.

Technology Is Changing How We Learn

In the last few years, virtual and hybrid education has grown very fast in Colombia. Programs like the one I am taking at SENA now offer virtual training, so students can study from home while they keep working or taking care of their families.

I have also noticed that more people are using artificial intelligence tools to study, practice languages, and organize their projects. I think that technology does not replace teachers, but it gives students more ways to learn, especially for people who cannot attend a classroom every day.

As a software student, I find this trend exciting, because it means that the tools I am learning to build today could help other students in the same situation as me in the future.

Critical Thinking & Media Literacy
Post 6 of 7

Thinking Before Sharing: Why Media Literacy Matters

Every day, we receive a huge amount of information through social media, videos, and messaging apps. Some of it is true, but some of it might be false or exaggerated. This is why critical thinking and media literacy are so important today: they help us decide what we can trust.

When I read a surprising piece of news, I try to ask a few questions first. Who wrote this? Could this source have a personal interest in the story? If a headline sounds too dramatic, it might not be telling the whole truth. As a person who works with technology, I know that information can be edited or taken out of context very easily.

"A society that thinks critically is less likely to be manipulated by false news, and that protects everyone."

I consider that schools, families, and companies should teach people how to verify information, not only how to find it.

Crime and Punishment
Post 7 of 7

Crime and Punishment in the Digital Age

As a software student, I am especially interested in a modern kind of crime: cybercrime. Every year, thousands of people are affected by data theft, online scams, and identity fraud. Personal information is stolen, bank accounts are emptied, and private photos are leaked, often before the victim even notices what has happened.

In many countries, new laws have been created to punish these crimes, and cybercriminals can be sent to prison or fined if they are caught. However, I think punishment alone is not enough. Software developers must also be trained to build safer systems, so fewer crimes can be committed in the first place.

"If future developers like me learn to code responsibly, fewer doors will be left open for criminals."

I consider that prevention and education are as important as punishment. If people understand how these crimes happen, they can protect themselves better.
